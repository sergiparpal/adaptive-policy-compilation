## 0. Predictions — bands and refutation lines

Drafted, unsigned. One row is one event. **A band's edge is its own refutation
line**, so band and refutation partition the axis and nothing can fall between
them.

**The runs.** `R = 3` runs per arm.
- **The loop.** Each run is `PLAN_REUSE.md`'s loop over the 2,000 cases of
  `generate_corpus(2000, seed=17)`, with a fresh engine of its arm, under
  `PLAN_CHANNEL.md`'s channel at coverage 1/2.
- **The draws.** Run `k` of either arm reads the draws of `PLAN_CHANNEL.md`'s
  `c r{k}`.
- **The order.** The arms alternate, as §2.5 sets.

**The statistics.** Each is a ratio of integer counts, a difference of such
ratios, or a count of runs. **Each is compared with its line exactly, as a
fraction.** No rounding enters a verdict.

- **`B`.** A run's final base, rebuilt from its record.
- **`space(B)` and `recall_q(B)`** are read as the run's own engine decides.
  - **In L** they are `PLAN_PLACEMENT.md`'s: a point is decided where its
    undefeated rules carry one queue. A point in CONFLICT counts as wrong.
  - **In K** they are the same, and a point in CONFLICT is decided by the queue
    the engine's last ranking puts first among its undefeated rules'.
  - **In both**, a point no rule matches counts as wrong.
- **`ranking(r)`.** The ranking K's engine held after the last birth of run
  `r`. It is recomputed from the record's rules, and §7 holds the two to each
  other.
  - **How it weighs a vote.** Every citation of a born rule's answer counts
    alike: whether or not its two rules decide a point between them, and however
    many citations one answer makes (§2.3).
  - **Where a pair's votes are wrong**, it follows them.
  - **How it meets containment and the edges.** It does not: it decides only
    where both leave a conflict (§2.2).
- **`taken(r)`.** Take the cases of K run `r` that the ranking decided: those
  at which rung 2's engine would have returned CONFLICT on the base as it stood.
  `taken(r)` is the share of them at which the engine's decision was the case's
  label. A decision the channel later reported is a wrong one.
- **`labels(r)`.** The reported errors of run `r`, in arrival order, each a
  ticket and its true queue. In both arms they reach the proposer.
- **`A(r)`.** The at-once asks from all of `labels(r)`. `space(A(r))` is the
  median of their `space`, each read as `PLAN_CORRECTIONS.md` §5.4 read them.
- **The median** of three runs is the middle one, and of two runs their mean.

## 5. The protocol

### 5.1 L: `PLAN_CORRECTIONS.md`'s arm L

Called, with nothing changed: `corrections.loop.run_loop` in that plan's arm
`l`, on a fresh `rung2.engine2.PriorityEngine`, with that plan's proposer, texts,
report and route of the label. `RK-g1` holds it to that plan's three L records,
served their answers.

### 5.2 K: the same loop on K's engine

K's run is the same call with one argument changed: the engine.
`ranked.engine.RankedEngine` is a subclass of `PriorityEngine` that overrides
`decide`, and nothing else rung 2's engine defines.
- **What it inherits.** `add`, `try_edge`, containment, the installed edges and
  the edge log. `placement.protocol.propose_v1c` reads them as it reads L's.
- **Its decision.** It asks rung 2's engine first. An ACTION or an IMPASSE is
  returned as it is. At a CONFLICT it returns an ACTION: of the undefeated
  rules, the one whose queue its ranking puts first.
- **Its votes** are read from the born rules as
  `corrections/declared.py`'s `citations` reads a record's: `beats`, `loses_to`
  and `dropped_edges`, a rule and a side.
- **Its ranking** is the earliest, in `itertools.permutations(ACTIONS)`, of the
  rankings that agree with the most votes, as `corrections/online.py`'s
  `Agreement` chooses it. `ranked.engine.Tally` keeps the count, and a reading
  of a finished record feeds its births through the same class.
- **When it counts.** Before it ranks, it takes the votes of every rule born
  since the last time. A birth's citations are complete by then.

### 5.3 The trigger

`channel.loop.escalates`, called: an IMPASSE or a CONFLICT escalates, and a
decision other than the case's label escalates as an error when its draw falls
below 1/2. In K no CONFLICT arises.

### 5.4 The at-once asks, and how an answer is read

`PLAN_CORRECTIONS.md` §5.3 and §5.4, called: the header, the lines, the request,
the ids, the validity of an answer and the installer. `ranked/ask.py` holds
this plan's sessions, and nothing else of its own. An at-once answer is
installed in its own list order, in both arms, as that plan read it.

### 5.5 What a run records, and how it resumes

`PLAN_CORRECTIONS.md` §5.6's record, under this plan's header, which carries the
arm, its engine and the engine's rule.
- **K's record also keeps**, under `ranked`:
  - the ranking after every birth, with its agreement, the rankings tied at it
    and the votes so far;
  - the last ranking, with its votes by pair of queues;
  - at every case, who decided: nobody at an impasse, rung 2's engine, or the
    ranking. Where the ranking decided, the queues of the undefeated rules, and
    the votes between the winner's queue and the best placed other.
- **`ranked/run.py`'s `ranked_check` holds every K record to its own births**
  after it is written: the log and the last ranking are what the record's rules
  give, every case has its row, and no case was a CONFLICT.
- **The resume** is that plan's. The partial file's header carries the arm and
  the engine's rule, so a run cannot resume under the other arm. A resumed K run
  counts its votes again from the births it replays.

## 6. Known traps

- **A K record read by rung 2's engine alone is another machine's.** Its
  conflicts would count as wrong, which is L's reading. `space(B)` in K takes
  the ranking at each conflict, and §0 says so. `ranked/reading.py` reads every
  base both ways, and `RK-g1` holds the reading over the space to the engine's
  own decisions at the arrivals.
- **The system prompt describes rung 2's engine.** It says an undeclared
  crossing escalates every time. In K it does not.
- **A decision K takes is not a decision K would take at the end.** The ranking
  moves as votes come. `RK-e` and the online figure read each decision as it
  was taken, and `space(B)` reads the last ranking.
- **The early ranking is the domain's order**, `T1_GENERAL` first, until votes
  come. The record counts the decisions taken on a tie.
- **A vote is counted once a citation, not once an answer.** An answer that
  places its newborn against twelve listed rules of one queue casts twelve
  votes. `PLAN_OVERLAP.md`'s lesson is that a rate over declared edges can rest
  on one declaration. §2.3 keeps the count the replay read.
- **A wrong ranking decides the same conflict at every arrival that falls
  there**, until a report brings a correction whose edge settles it. `taken(r)`
  weighs a conflict by the arrivals it decides.
- **A correction may not decide its own ticket.** A rule nested inside it, often
  the rule that erred, keeps the ticket by containment, in K as in L.
- **A rule of another queue nested in the keyword's region** takes
  `SECURITY_INCIDENT`'s points whatever the ranking says. `RK-c` reads what is
  left.
- **K may spin.** Where a correction's own edge is refused, its ticket stays in
  conflict, the ranking decides it the same way, and the next report writes the
  correction again. That is how a K run could reach the cap, and how the count
  of §2.3 could repeat.
- **"Level 1" means three things in this tree.** See the preamble.
- **The labelled tickets are each arm's own errors.** They differ between the
  arms, so `A(r)` is asked of different labels in K and in L.
- **An at-once answer is a list**, read in its own order in both arms. It is
  not read by K's engine.
- **One ticket's story can mislead.** Check it against the run's other tickets of
  its region before citing it.
- **A median of three runs moves on one run.** *Thin* says so, as §0 sets.

## 7. Blocking checks, before any record is written

`ranked/gates.py`. They run before every write and every spend, and in every dry
run. **They pass before the signature.**

- **`RK-g1`: the arms are what they claim.**
  - The suite is green, and `PLAN_CORRECTIONS.md`'s own `CR-g1` to `CR-g3` pass.
  - On each of that plan's three L runs, served its answers by
    `channel.gates.Replay`:
    - **arm `l` gives back the run**: its rules with their citations, its edge
      log, and every case's outcome, winner, queue and cause. A label is handed
      at each reported error, and nowhere else;
    - **arm `k`'s engine with rung 2's decision put back gives back the same
      record**, and the same messages at every call. So the arms differ in the
      decision alone;
    - **its ranking after every birth is `online.json`'s**, and its votes at the
      end are `declared.json`'s;
    - **on the run's final base, its own decision at every corpus case is rung
      2's**, but at the cases rung 2's engine leaves in CONFLICT. There it is the
      first queue of its ranking among the undefeated rules', as
      `ranked/reading.py` reads the base over the space.
  - That holds at the three runs' 2,123 calls, 660 reported errors and 392
    conflict cases.
  - K's engine overrides `decide`, and nothing else rung 2's engine defines.
- **`RK-g2`: the texts, the requests and the declared figures.**
  - Both arms send `PLAN_CORRECTIONS.md`'s arm L's five texts, by digest, and its

  - The at-once asks send that plan's texts and its request with their budget.
  - §0's figures 2 to 7 come back.
- **`RK-g3`: the schedule and the instruments.**

  - Before any vote, the ranking is the domain's order.
  - **Step 0**, `EXTERNAL_REVIEW.md` §1.1's method. Under the transcriber and

## 13. What this plan cannot settle, declared before it runs

1. **Which part of K's effect is the decision, and which the rules it leads
   to.** The arms differ in one decision, and everything after it follows. The
   two cross-readings of `RK-g` are replays.
2. **Whether telling the proposer how K's engine settles a conflict would change
   its votes.** The system prompt describes rung 2's engine in both arms.
3. **A rule of another queue born nested in a queue's region.** A ranking does
   not undo containment, and a guard that reads no truth takes the loop's own
   exceptions with its errors. The lever left is in the births: a first call
   shown the rule that erred. That is another plan.
4. **Another count of the votes**: each pair of regions once, one vote an
   answer, or one weighted by what each pair decides. Step 0 cannot tell them
   apart under this engine, and none is run.
5. **An engine that keeps abstaining where its votes are silent**: one that
   returns CONFLICT for a pair of queues with no vote, or an even one. K
   decides every conflict, on a tie rule.
6. **A ranking of the whole base.** The first draft's engine failed Step 0 and
   is set aside. Whether some loop could live with one is not asked.
7. **The ranking without labels.** K runs at level 1 with report R1. The same
   engine under arm N's configuration, or at level 3, is not run.
8. **Whether the report or the label carries what L and K share.**
   `PLAN_CORRECTIONS.md` §13's first item stands.
9. **A paired effect.** Three pairs of runs cannot carry a sign test. Every row
   is a band reading.
10. **One model, one provider, one manual level, one coverage, one arrival
    order, and three runs an arm.**
