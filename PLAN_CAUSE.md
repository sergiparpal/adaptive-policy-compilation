**The item.** Told that the base is not shown, the loop's first call writes its
ticket's governing region about half as often as told that the base is empty.
`PLAN_HALVES.md` found the cut in that sentence, and its POST-RUN reading found
what goes with it.

- **What `PLAN_HALVES.md` measured.** In one period, on DeepInfra, at 136 tickets
  the reasoning loops had escalated, with level 3's manual and reasoning on:
  - the empty base wrote the region whole in 0.7731 of its right-queue answers,
    and v2b's paragraph in 0.3600 (`HV-b` holds);
  - v2b's first half alone, the base not shown and a rule asked for this ticket,
    wrote it in 0.3008 (`HV-c` refuted, `HV-d` holds);
  - at the rarest queue's seven tickets, dropping the overlap clause gave
    nothing back (`HV-f` refuted).
- **What the reading found** (`FINDINGS_HALVES.md`, *Why the hidden half cuts*).
  Every first call is told the engine could not decide its ticket.
  - **Told that the base is not shown**, the model supposes the engine holds
    rules like the manual's. It finds the ticket in the governing rule and in a
    crossing rule of another queue, and reads the impasse as their conflict.
  - **It settles that conflict** by a cut inside both, or by sides declared.
    149 of the 162 held-out cuts are the governing region cut by the first
    crossing rule the ticket matches.
  - **Told that the base is empty**, it mostly says the engine has no rule yet,
    and writes the governing rule's region.
  - **What it could not say.** The codes say which account goes with the cut,
    not that the account caused it. On the pairwise answers the model named the
    broader rule *"against what its own explanations say"*
    (`EXTERNAL_REVIEW.md` §2).
- **What no first call is told: why its ticket escalated.** The first call is the
  same message at an impasse, at a conflict and at a reported error
  (`reasoned_loop/protocol.py`, `Resumable.first`). Its system prompt says
  *"El motor simbolico no ha podido decidir este ticket"*, and its user message
  heads the ticket *"TICKET EN IMPASSE"*, at every escalation. At a reported
  error both are false: the engine decided the ticket, and a report said it
  erred.
- **Why it matters for the loop.** The cut is where the rarest queue is lost.
  Under v1c the cuts cost mostly `H04`'s and `H05`'s points, whose tickets
  escalate too rarely for a later draft to write their region whole
  (`FINDINGS_PLACEMENT.md`, *What the cuts cost*).

**None of this is new analysis, and the plan's part is the instrument.** The
reading is the analysis, and it is POST-RUN. What this plan adds is the cause
told, beside v2b's paragraph and the empty base, in one period, of one provider,
with bands signed before any of its figures exists.

**The two notices.** A loop knows why each ticket escalated: rung 2's engine
returns an impasse when no rule matches, and the channel reports an error the
engine made. A blind loop can tell its first call either without showing it the
base. Each notice stands after v2b's paragraph, as `corrections/protocol.py`
sets its report after it. §2.1 gives the exact bytes.

- **The impasse notice** says that no rule of the base matches the ticket, and
  that this is why the engine could not decide it. It is new.
- **The review notice** says that the ticket is not an impasse: the engine
  decided it, and a reviewer marked the decision wrong. It is
  `PLAN_CORRECTIONS.md`'s report, which that plan's arm L and both arms of
  `PLAN_RANKED.md` sent at level 1, cut before the sentence that names the right
  queue.

**Four arms.** Each arm is the loop's first call at a ticket, byte for byte but
for what stands where the base would be.

- **`v`, v2b's paragraph.** Its prompts are `PLAN_HALVES.md`'s arm `v`'s, byte for
  byte: the first call every v1b and v1c loop sends.
- **`e`, the empty base.** Its prompts are `PLAN_HALVES.md`'s arm `e`'s, byte for
  byte.
- **`i`, v2b's paragraph and the impasse notice.**
- **`r`, v2b's paragraph and the review notice.**

**The question, in its cleanest form.** At the tickets `PLAN_HALVES.md` asked,
with level 3's manual in the system prompt and reasoning on, of one provider and
in one period:

- does the paragraph's gap hold in this period?
- told that no rule of the base matches its ticket, does the first call write
  the region as an empty base makes it, or does it still cut?
- does it stop reading the impasse as a conflict between two of the manual's
  rules?
- told that the base decided its ticket and a reviewer marked the decision
  wrong, does it write the region less often than told that no rule matches?
- at the rarest queue's seven tickets, where every recorded escalation was a
  reported error or a conflict, does the review notice give the region back?

**What it changes in what is measured, said first.**

- **It installs nothing.** No loop runs, no rule is born and no base grows. Every
  row reads the first call's answer alone, classed as `PLAN_HALVES.md`'s Stage C
  classed it.
- **`v` and `e` are asked again, and `i` and `r` for the first time.** No loop has
  sent either notice. `PLAN_CORRECTIONS.md`'s and `PLAN_RANKED.md`'s loops sent
  the review notice's words at level 1, with the right queue after them, and
  never at level 3 or without it.
- **Only `v`, `i` and `r` can be sent in a blind loop.** A loop's base is empty
  only before its first birth, and a blind first call never says so. So `e` is
  diagnostic, the reference the notices are read against.
- **Each notice is asked at every ticket, whatever its cause in the loops.** A
  loop would send the impasse notice only at an impasse, and the review notice
  only at a reported error.
  - **Each is true of some base at every ticket.** Any ticket is an impasse
    before a rule covers it, and an error once a rule of the wrong queue covers
    it. Here no base exists, and each notice describes one the model cannot see.
  - **So `W_i` and `W_r` describe the notices at `T`, not a loop's mix of them.**
    `CA-g` reports each at the tickets where the DeepInfra loops met its cause.
- **The review notice contradicts the frame.** The system prompt says the engine
  could not decide the ticket, and the ticket is headed *"TICKET EN IMPASSE"*.
  The notice says it is not an impasse. Every loop's error escalation already
  sends that frame at a ticket the engine decided. The notice says what
  happened, and how the model reconciles the two is part of what is measured.
- **Whole is breadth, not correctness.** The share reads an answer's extension
  against its ticket's governing region. *"Narrow ≠ correct"*
  (`ARBITRATION_REPORT.md` §2, which `CHAT_SUMMARY.md` §1 repeats), and whole is
  not right either. No row reads whether a base decides right.
- **At a reported error, whole may not win in a loop.** The rule that erred is
  often nested inside the governing region. A correction drawn whole around it
  loses its own ticket to it by containment, and v1c's round never lists a
  nested rule (`FINDINGS_CORRECTIONS.md`, *Why the final base forgets its
  corrections*). So a review notice that makes the model cut on purpose, to win
  by containment, would read here as a cut. `W_r` reads breadth, and whether a
  whole draft at an error would decide its ticket is the loop plan's question.
- **A share can rise by construction.** `W` counts a draft wider than its part as
  whole, and leaves out of its denominator the answers of the wrong queue and
  those naming no valid rule. An arm can raise its share by drafting wider than
  the governing region, or by losing the right queue where it would have cut.
  `CA-g` reports both, by arm, and a hold carried by either is read as such.
- **A count can move without the operation.** Rung 2's instruction not to cut
  *"moved the numbers (1.6% → 7.25%) but not the operation"* (`CHAT_SUMMARY.md`
  §1). `CA-g` reports every answer's class, a cut by a listed rule or another
  narrowing, so that a moved share can be read as a moved operation or not.
- **`CA-f` reads the account with `PLAN_HALVES.md`'s codebook, frozen.**
  `halves/hidden.py` froze it on that plan's pass 1 and its clauses held on the
  held-out answers. It is called and not edited. Here it reads answers whose
  prompts carry a notice it never saw. Its `BETWEEN` code matches a conflict
  named between two of the manual's identifiers, and neither notice names one.
- **Reasoning is on**, as in every loop since `PLAN_REASONED_LOOP.md`, against
  `PLAN_REUSE.md` §1's amendment, for this plan's calls only.
- **Hard rule 5 allows two new texts.** `v` and `e` have recorded results. The
  review notice is a recorded text cut before its label. The impasse notice is
  new: two short sentences in the review notice's form. Their bytes are fixed
  before signature (§2.1), and after it nothing in them moves.
- **Every adjudicated row is read within one provider and one period** but
  `CA-a`, the instrument row, which sets today's `v` beside `PLAN_HALVES.md`'s on
  the same provider, two weeks apart. Every other comparison across dates is
  reported in `CA-g`.

**The asks, the classes and the notation.**

- **A prompt is an arm at a ticket**: 4 × 136 = 544 prompts.
- **Pass 1 and pass 2** each ask every prompt once, the arms interleaved (§2.3).
  **The rarest queue's session** asks each of the 28 prompts at `T₇` four times

- **An answer is valid** when the call came back, after its retries, with a
  payload that parses, from the pinned provider. A failed call, an answer cut
  off past repair and an answer from another provider are invalid. They are
  counted and reported, and never classed. An empty object parses: it is valid,
  and classed *no rule*.
- **A valid answer's class** is `PLAN_HALVES.md`'s: `placement/cost.py`'s

    region holds the ticket.

    its part lies in its extension. A draft wider than the part is whole too.
- **`W_x`**, for arm `x` over a set of answers, is `H_x / R_x`.
  - `R_x` counts its valid answers of the right queue that name a valid rule:
    every class but *wrong queue* and *no rule*.
  - `H_x` counts those classed *whole*.
- **`B_x`** is `K_x / R_x`, over the same answers. `K_x` counts those whose
  reasoning carries `halves/hidden.py`'s `BETWEEN` code: a conflict named between
  two listed rules, *"between H.. and H.."* or *"entre H.. y H.."*.
- **No superscript** reads both passes, at every ticket of `T`. **A superscript
  `⁷`** reads both passes and the rarest queue's session, at the tickets of
  `T₇`.

**The unit, the error and *thin*.** All of it is settled here and not in the
scorer, which is where `PLAN_AUTHORSHIP.md`'s `E-c` lost its verdict.

- **Every row pools the answers it reads**, over its tickets and its sessions.
  The ticket is the cluster: its answers share its region and its governing
  rule.
- **Every statistic is a ratio of integer counts, and is compared with its line
  exactly, as a fraction.** No rounding enters a verdict.
- **The standard error is clustered by ticket.**
  - **The formula.** For a statistic `Σ c_x W_x` over arms, ticket `t`
    contributes `u_t = Σ c_x (h_{x,t} − W_x r_{x,t}) / R_x`, where `h_{x,t}`
    and `r_{x,t}` are its counts. The variance is `C/(C−1) · Σ_t u_t²` over the
    row's `C` tickets. `CA-f` reads `B` the same way, with `k_{x,t}` for
    `h_{x,t}`.
  - **`CA-a`'s statistic is a mean over tickets**, and its error is that mean's.
  - **It is a label.** It is a float, and it marks *thin* and never decides.
- **A verdict is labelled *thin*** in either of two cases, and the label does not
  change the verdict:
  - the statistic lies within one standard error of its line, or of the nearer
    edge of `CA-a`'s band;
  - read on pass 1 alone or on pass 2 alone, it falls on the other side. For
    `CA-e`, read with any one of `T₇`'s tickets left out, it falls on the other
    side.

## 5. Known traps

- **The review notice contradicts the frame.** The system prompt says the engine
  could not decide the ticket, and the ticket is headed *"TICKET EN IMPASSE"*,
  as at every loop's error escalation. The notice says it is not an impasse. The
  reasoning is kept whole, and `CA-g` reports what the model does with the two.
- **Each notice is asked where a loop would not send it.** §0 says so first.
  `CA-g` reads each at the tickets where the DeepInfra loops met its cause, and
  those tickets are fewer than `T`.
- **A whole draft at a reported error may lose its ticket in a loop.** §0 says so
  first. No row here reads it.
- **A wider draft holds its part too.** `CA-g` reports breadth, so a hold carried
  by drafts wider than the region can be seen.
- **The texts could leak the policy in code.** The notices name neither a queue
  nor a rule, and reach the proposer through the frozen route only.
  `tests/test_oracle_separation.py` is to watch the package's proposer-path
  modules, as the authority channel.
- **The classes read the policy.** Stage C reads each ticket's governing rule and
  the true queue of every point. The asker reads no label, no class and no
  policy.
- **The codebook was frozen on answers without a notice.** A reasoning that
  quotes the notice quotes no rule identifier, so it cannot match `BETWEEN` by
  quoting. `CONFLICT` reads a window after the impasse is mentioned, and the
  impasse notice mentions it: §0 reads `BETWEEN` for that reason, and `CA-g`
  reports the other codes.
- **An answer cut off at 16,000 tokens is invalid** unless its retry parses.
- **DeepInfra's answers at one ticket change from ask to ask**
  (`FINDINGS_PLACEMENT.md`, `FINDINGS_HALVES.md`). The passes and the rarest
  queue's session measure that spread, which a single ask a ticket would hide.
- **The first call may still declare against the manual's identifiers.** The
  system prompt lets `beats` and `loses_to` cite rules of *"la lista de
  arriba"*, and the manual's list is above. Under `v`, 133 of 300 valid answers
  declared, every one against the manual's identifiers. `CA-g` counts them.
- **Check the asker's validity flag against §0's before the first pass**
  (`PLAN_HALVES.md`'s lesson). An empty object is valid, and classed *no rule*. A
  test runs the flag over one before signature.
- **A test must not pin whether the plan is signed** (`PLAN_APPLY.md`'s lesson).
  Pin the count of signature lines, and the gate's verdicts on fake plans.
- **A float recomputed on 3.10 can differ from 3.12's in the last place.**
  Verdicts are fractions. A check that compares a recomputed float with a record
  uses a tolerance.

**[Added before signature, 2026-11-25] Found writing the package.**

## 6. Blocking checks, before any record is written

`cause/gates.py`. They run before any record of the plan is written, and any
failure stops the plan before a figure exists. They print whether each check
passes, and no figure of any plan.

- **`CA-g1`, the texts.**
  - **`e` and `v` are `PLAN_HALVES.md`'s.** At every one of their 328 asks, the
    digest that plan's Stage A record froze, and its three sessions' 600 rows of
    `e` and `v` asked. That record's system prompt and paragraphs are this plan's.
  - **`v` is the loop's first call**, `reasoned_loop.protocol.messages_for`, at
    every ticket.
  - **The notices.** `i` and `r` are `v`, a blank line and their notice, as
    `corrections.protocol.base_text` sets its report at every queue. The review
    notice is `REPORT` up to the sentence that names the queue, with one newline
    in place of one space. Neither notice names a queue or a rule, or keeps a
    placeholder, and the four paragraphs differ.
  - **One paragraph apart.** Every arm's user message is `v`'s with the paragraph
    replaced, under the loop's system prompt, and every repetition asks its
    prompt's messages.
  - **No builder takes a row, and the digests have teeth**: a changed character
    moves one.
  - **The suite is green.**
- **`CA-g2`, the request and the instrument.**
  - **The request** is `PLAN_HALVES.md`'s, `placement.protocol.request_with` over
    the loop's request with DeepInfra's pin, on the first attempt and on a retry.
    That plan's three session records carry the same model, temperature,
    settings and pin.
  - **No module on the asker's path names a label**, in a name or a string
    literal, as `N-g2` reads them: `cause/plan.py`, `prompts.py`,
    `population.py` and `ask.py`.
  - **The instrument gives back `PLAN_HALVES.md`'s records.** `halves/score.py`,
    run over that plan's records, gives back its six verdicts, fraction for
    fraction, and its classes by arm. `halves/hidden.py`'s codebook gives back
    its counts on that plan's pass 1. The standard errors are floats, and are not
    compared.
- **`CA-g3`, the design.**
  - **The tickets** are `PLAN_HALVES.md`'s, as its Stage A record keeps them, and
    every one met a cause in the 24 runs. The seven are the corpus's tickets of
    `ONCALL_ESCALATION` by level 3's list, read over the space.
  - **The asks.** 544 prompts, 136 an arm; each pass every prompt once, in its
    order rebuilt from `ORDER_SEED`; the rarest queue's session its 28 prompts

    seven.
  - **The partial files are ignored by git.**
  - **The asker's validity flag is §0's.** Built by the asker's own `row_of`, an
    empty object and a rule from the pinned provider are valid. A failed call, a
    payload that is not an object and another provider's answer are not.
- **`CA-g4`, the signature.** The gate reads `PLAN_CAUSE.md` and no other plan,
  and counts every signature line. It does not block a dry run, which writes
  nothing; it blocks every write.

**What the tests pin, beside the checks** (`tests/test_cause.py`): the constants

## 8. Stage B — the asks (gated on §0)

`cause/ask.py` writes `results_cause/ask_<session>.json`, one session at a time:
the smoke run, then pass 1, pass 2 and the rarest queue's session.

- **The order of events**: the signature; the turn, each session after the one
  before it, and the passes after a smoke record whose every arm holds a valid
  answer and whose every attempt that reached the model was served by the pin,
  reasoned and ended `stop`; the key, read off OpenRouter's key endpoint, a
  management key refused; `CA-g1` to `CA-g3`; Stage A's record holding the
  design the checks rebuilt; the record guard; and only then the client.
- **A session that dies keeps what it paid for**, in its partial file, and the
  same command resumes there. Five failed asks in a row, or a refusal for the
  rate through every wait, stop it unrecorded.
- **Who launches them.** The agent, as Sergi chose (§2.5), through a supervisor
  detached from the agent's harness.
  - **What it does.** It runs the sessions one at a time, in the order above,
    and commits each record alone, through the pre-commit hook.
  - **Pushing.** It pushes each record to one pull request. It retries a push
    that times out, and goes on if the push still fails: the next push carries
    the commit.
  - **Resuming.** It resumes a session an outage stopped. WSL stops the
    supervisor with the session. The agent then relaunches it, and the session
    resumes from its partial file, every kept answer with its own seconds.
  - **What it never does.** It never passes the overwrite flag, never skips the
    hook, and never merges: the merge waits for Sergi's word.
  - **Checking it is up.** A check that matches its process by command line runs
    apart from the command that launches it.
  - **Its messages** tell a launch a blocking check refused, which kept nothing,
    from one that resumed from the partial file.
- **What the commits may show** (§0): each session's asks, failures, retries
  and seconds, and no class, share, code or row.
