**The item.** The loop writes its narrow rules as cuts, and the cuts cost the
rarest queue.

- **The cut.** Where a ticket matches, after its governing rule, a listed rule
  whose region does not contain the governing rule's, about seven narrow births
  in ten are the governing rule's region cut by one listed rule.
  - It is 118 of 167 in `PLAN_PLACEMENT.md`'s six runs on DeepInfra, and 124 of
    165 and 69 of 102 in `PLAN_CHANNEL.md`'s and `PLAN_REASONED_LOOP.md`'s on
    Baidu.
  - Nine cuts in ten, about, are by the first such rule, and by a rule of another
    queue (`FINDINGS_PLACEMENT.md`, *Whether the cut is why the loop's births are
    narrow*).
- **Why the proposer cuts.** Level 3's system prompt says: *"Por eso solo
  necesitas declarar prioridad frente a reglas que se solapan con la tuya sin
  que una contenga a la otra. Si tu regla es un caso particular de otra, no
  declares nada: el nivel 1 ya lo resuelve."* A cut is a particular case of the
  lower rule it would conflict with.
  - The notes at the rarest queue's tickets say so. In translation, at case 1432
    of v1c r3: *"Automatic containment prioritises it over H04 and H07, without
    any need to declare priority"* (`FINDINGS_PLACEMENT.md`, *Why the runs under
    run 3's draws lost `ONCALL_ESCALATION`*).
  - **It is a form the record already names.** `ARBITRATION_REPORT.md` §2 gives
    rung 1's failure in general form: *"specificity works if and only if the
    exceptions are **refinements** of the rules they override"*, and it inverts
    on *"a layer of **broad overrides** on top —security, compliance,
    on-call—"*. A cut is the proposer writing an on-call override as a refinement
    of a lower rule, the one shape containment ranks first.
  - **And the record once read it the other way.** The same report's §5 counted,
    in August, that the proposer *"correctly identifies that subsumption resolves
    a nesting with no need for a declaration"*, as a sign of capability. Its §3
    calls level 1 *"the only one no proposer can fake"*; a cut is a proposer
    choosing the extension level 1 then ranks first, which its §2's *"free
    conditional on a discipline of authorship"* already allows for. Its §5's
    note of 2026-10-06 records that v1 told the proposer not to declare on nested
    pairs.
- **What it costs.** A cut decides only its own points.
  - At a rule whose tickets escalate often, a later draft writes the region
    whole. Of `PLAN_PLACEMENT.md`'s 80 rule-runs with a cut, 58 also had a whole
    draft born, and lost 4,445 points of the space.
  - At a rule whose tickets escalate one to three times a run, the cut is the
    region's only draft. The 10 rule-runs where every draft was a cut lost
    15,720 points (`FINDINGS_PLACEMENT.md`, *What the cuts cost*).
  - **Under v1c that is the rarest queue.** `H04`'s and `H05`'s points are 12,560
    of the 15,215 v1c's three bases lose. Outside them a base loses under one
    point in a hundred of the space.
- **So the rarest queue is lost at the first call.** Under run 3's draws the
  channel supplied the queue, and the proposer drafted the right queue at every
  escalation of it. No draft at a ticket of `H04` was `H04`'s region whole, in
  eleven. On the bridge, both providers cut at two of the queue's tickets, and
  DeepInfra cut at two more where Baidu did not (`FINDINGS_PLACEMENT.md`,
  *Whether the cut is the provider's*).

## 0. Predictions — bands and refutation lines

Drafted, unsigned. One row is one event. **A band's edge is its own refutation
line**, so band and refutation partition the axis and nothing can fall between
them.

**The runs.** `R = 3` runs per arm. Each run is `PLAN_REUSE.md`'s loop over the
2,000 cases of `generate_corpus(2000, seed=17)`, with a fresh rung 2 engine. Its
trigger is `PLAN_CHANNEL.md`'s channel at coverage 1/2. Run `k` of either arm
reads the draws of `PLAN_CHANNEL.md`'s `c r{k}`, as `PLAN_PLACEMENT.md`'s did. The
arms alternate, as §2.3 sets.

**The statistics.** Each is a ratio of integer counts. **Each is compared with its
line exactly, as a fraction.** No rounding enters a verdict.

- **`B`, `space(B)`, `recall_q(B)`, `calls(r)` and `refused(r)`** are
  `PLAN_PLACEMENT.md`'s, computed by the same functions, called.
  - **`B`** is a run's final base, rebuilt from the record and held to the
    engine the run ended with.
  - **`space(B)`** is the share of the 134,400 points that `B` decides right
    under rung 2's engine. A CONFLICT or an IMPASSE is not right.
  - **`recall_q(B)`** is the share of the points whose true queue is `q` that
    `B` sends to `q`.
  - **`refused(r)`** is the share of run `r`'s escalations that ended *unplaced*
    with an answer: the placement call came back with the draft's rule and left
    a listed rule without a side. The denominator is every escalation of the
    run.
- **`whole(r)`.** Of run `r`'s first-call drafts of the right queue, the share
  that hold their ticket's part whole.
  - **A draft's part** is the points of its ticket's governing rule's region whose
    true queue is that rule's, as `placement/births.py` reads a birth's. The
    governing rule is the first listed rule whose region holds the ticket.
  - **A draft holds it** when every one of those points lies in the draft's
    extension. A draft wider than the part holds it too.
  - **Drafts of another queue, and drafts naming no valid rule,** are left out of
    the share, above and below. They are counted and reported.
  - **It is read on the first call's answer, born or not**, by
    `placement/cost.py`'s `draft_class`, called. The first call is what the text
    changes.
- **The median** of three runs is the middle one, and of two runs their mean.

**The unit, *thin*, and when a row has no value.** All of it is settled here and
not in the scorer, which is where `PLAN_AUTHORSHIP.md`'s `E-c` lost its verdict.

- **Every adjudicated row reads a median over runs**: over an arm's runs, or, for
  `WR-f`, over pairs of runs under one set of draws.
- **A verdict on a median is labelled *thin*** when any one run of an arm the
  row reads, put in place of that arm's median, would put the statistic on the
  other side of its line. That is `PLAN_MANUAL.md`'s test, as
  `PLAN_PLACEMENT.md` applied it. The label does not change the verdict.
- **What a spread between runs as wide as a line does.**
  - `PLAN_PLACEMENT.md`'s v1c bases decided 0.9510 to 0.9683 of the space, a
    spread of 0.0173. That is less than `WR-d`'s line of 1/40, so one run can
    move `WR-d` only near its line.
  - Its v1c runs recalled 1/15 to 8/15 of the rarest queue, so `WR-e` may be
    thin whichever way it falls.
  - Its v1c runs refused 1/17 to 5/49 of their escalations. A ratio of medians
    moves with one run of either arm, so `WR-c` may be thin too.
  - No line was widened to absorb a spread, and none will be.
- **What run 2's draws do to `WR-b`, `WR-e` and `WR-f`.** The draws of run 2 make
  no `H05` ticket reportable, and `H05` governs a third of the queue's points.
  Under them the transcriber recalls 2/3 of the queue, so no run under them
  reaches 0.90 unless the proposer writes `H05`'s region from another ticket.
  - **`WR-b`** then holds only if v1d's runs 1 and 3 both reach its line.
  - **`WR-e`** is then refuted only if v1c's runs 1 and 3 both reach it. Neither
    did in `PLAN_PLACEMENT.md`, where v1c peaked at 8/15. So a hold of `WR-e` says
    little more than the draws do, and it weighs little as a replication.
  - **`WR-f`** pairs the runs by their draws, so each difference sets the two
    arms on the same supply of the queue.
- **A run is complete** when it reaches case 2,000, or the cap of §1.
  - A run that cannot be completed leaves its arm with fewer runs. The rows read
    the runs that exist, and the record names the missing one.
  - A row that reads an arm with fewer than two runs has no value.
  - **`WR-c` has no value if v1c's median `refused(r)` is 0.**
  - **`WR-f` reads the pairs of runs that both exist**, run `k` of v1d with run
    `k` of v1c. With fewer than two pairs it has no value. Its *thin* is the same
    test read on the pairs: the verdict is thin when any one pair's difference,
    put in place of the median, would fall on the other side of the line.
  - **`WR-a` reads the v1d runs that have a `whole(r)`.** A run with no
    first-call draft of the right queue has none, which no run of
    `PLAN_PLACEMENT.md` has had. With fewer than two such runs the row has no
    value.
- **A row with no value is unadjudicable**, and the record says so.
- **A run that meets a long refusal for the rate** waits it out and, once every
  wait is spent, stops unrecorded; the same command resumes it from its partial
  file (§5.4). It is one run, from its first launch.
- **What the runs' commits may show.** The supervisor commits each record with a
  message written from it (§2.5).
  - **What may show:** each run's escalations, its calls, its verdicts by kind and
    its rounds sent. So it may give `refused(r)`'s counts, `WR-c`'s inputs.
  - **What may not:** no message gives `whole(r)`, a base's `space`, a recall or
    a ratio, and none is computed before Stage C.
  - **What the findings do:** they name what was seen before Stage C.

## 6. Known traps

**6.1 — Every error escalation is told it is an impasse.** v1 calls the model the
system's exception component, and the user message labels the ticket *TICKET EN
IMPASSE*. On an error escalation both are false. Both arms keep the prompts
(`PLAN_CHANNEL.md` §6.1).

**6.2 — Level 3 hands over the label** (`PLAN_MANUAL.md` §6.2). Every row
measures what the loop compiles from a policy it holds as text, and T1 points at
that text's own rule.

**6.3 — The trigger reads the truth.** In a deployment the report comes from the
world. Here the corpus labels stand in for it.

**6.4 — The online figures are corrected online.** A reported error's decision is
replaced by the proposer's answer. They are reported, and no row reads them.

**6.5 — The draws fix which cases can be reported, not which are.** The bases part
from the first draft one arm writes and the other does not, so the pairing takes
the channel's luck out of the contrast and does not make the runs replicas.

**6.6 — `SECURITY_INCIDENT` weighs 3/8 of the space.** `space(B)` moves with its
recall more than with any other queue's. Every space figure in `WR-g` is split by
the keyword.

**6.7 — The text could leak the policy in code.** It names neither a queue nor a
rule, and it reaches the proposer through the frozen route only.
`tests/test_oracle_separation.py` watches the package's proposer-path modules.

**6.8 — A whole draft of a default is the default.** At a ticket `H29` governs,
the region whole is the catch-all. It is born, and containment puts every
narrower rule above it, as the transcriber's run does.

**6.9 — `whole(r)` reads the first call, not the birth.** A draft that holds its
part and is refused counts in `WR-a`, and its refusal counts in `WR-c`.

**6.10 — One possessive in T1 can be read two ways.** In *"una regla dentro de
otra solo le gana a esa otra, y el resto de su region se queda con reglas de
otras colas"*, *su region* is meant as the region the first sentence asks for,
the governing rule's. It could be read as the inner rule's or the lower rule's.
The text was chosen as written and is not changed. The notes and the reasoning
are kept whole, and `WR-a` reads what the drafts did.

**6.11 — The system prompt's level-1 sentence stays.** It tells the proposer that
a rule which is a particular case of another needs no declaration, and T1 tells it
not to narrow its rule to make it one. The two agree at the lower rules that
contain the governing rule's region, and pull apart only at those that cross it
(§0, figure 5). Both are sent, in that order, at every first call.

**6.12 — A whole draft cannot take back what a narrower rule of another queue
holds.** A rule of another queue born before it, whose extension lies strictly
inside the draft's, beats it there by containment, right or wrong: *"Narrow ≠
correct"* (`ARBITRATION_REPORT.md` §2).
- **Nothing the round can do moves it.** v1c's round never lists such a rule, and
  no side against it installs anything.
- **So an earlier wrong rule keeps its points.** A narrow rule of another queue,
  written at one of the queue's tickets before the whole draft came, keeps them
  whatever the whole draft declares.
- **The transcriber never meets one**, because it never writes a wrong rule.
- **What `WR-g` counts.** By governing rule with a whole birth, the points the
  rule governs that lie inside such rules, and of the points the final base loses
  there, those that do. A point governed by a rule is one whose first match it
  is, so the counts add up over rules.

**6.13 — A whole draft covers more than its part, and can be wider still.** The
governing rule's region holds points earlier rules govern, of other queues. There
a whole draft is beaten by containment where an earlier rule's draft lies inside
it, and by a declared edge where one crosses it. A draft wider than the region
holds its part too (§0), and `WR-g` reports each first-call draft's breadth.
`WR-d` and `WR-g`'s lost points read what both cost.

**6.14 — Asked about two rules, the model favoured the broader.** It *"names the
**broader** rule 0.6038 of the time"* when it judged pairs (`EXTERNAL_REVIEW.md`
§2). In a round under v1d the draft is often the broader rule of each listed pair.
`WR-g` reads the placed edges' direction against the better rule, as `RL-e` read
it, in both arms.

**6.15 — A round whose list is empty is not sent**, under v1c's rule, and a rule
left out still matters to cycles: an edge never asked for never enters the graph
the engine searches when it refuses an edge for closing one (`PLAN_PLACEMENT.md`
§6.11).

**6.16 — A median of three runs moves on one run.** *Thin* says so, as §0 sets.

**6.17 — Back-to-back runs leave `git_dirty` true**, because each finds the
earlier records not yet committed. The supervisor commits each record before the
next run, and `code_dirty` is what identifies the code.

**6.18 — A new period for v1c.** This plan's v1c runs are `PLAN_PLACEMENT.md`'s
arm on new dates, on the same provider. `WR-e` replicates `PL-f` on them, and
`WR-g` sets them beside that plan's runs under the same draws.

## 13. What this plan cannot settle, declared before it runs

1. **What a proposer without the list drafts.** At level 3 the instruction asks
   for the list's rule. T2, or a lower level, is another plan.
2. **Whether the round's refusals or the text decide a refutation of `WR-b`**,
   if `WR-a` and `WR-c` both fail.
3. **The partial round.** §2.2 keeps v1c's.
4. **Which of the two texts the proposer follows where they pull apart.** The
   system prompt's level-1 sentence and T1 are sent together, and no arm sends T1
   without the sentence.
5. **A right edge refused for a cycle.** v1d changes what is drafted, not the
   rule that refuses an edge. `WR-g` counts the right ones refused.
6. **Whether the text or the list's length decides `WR-c`.** The text changes the
   draft, and the draft changes the list it meets. A rise in refusals may be the
   length's and not the proposer's reading of the text. `WR-g` sets the refusals
   beside the lists' lengths in both arms, and adjudicates nothing.
7. **Which of the three candidates `ARBITRATION_REPORT.md` §5 leaves open** for
   why the proposer partitions: the framing of the task, the model, or eliciting
   by rule-writing. v1d holds all three fixed.
8. **The supply of rare tickets.** The draws are `PLAN_CHANNEL.md`'s, so run 2
   cannot report `H05` in either arm.
9. **The comparison across plans.** This plan's v1c runs, set beside
   `PLAN_PLACEMENT.md`'s, cross dates.
10. **One model, one provider, one manual, one arrival order, one coverage, and
    three runs an arm.**
